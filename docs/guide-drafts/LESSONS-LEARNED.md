# Lessons learned the hard way

*Draft 1, 27 Oct 2026, from jon's first interview answers. Grows over time: interviews, then rules
drawn from Problems and retros, each confirmed by jon before it goes in. To paste into Staff
Documents and share with Claude (topics: Client questions, Quoting) once it has a few more entries.*

Each lesson is a rule, why we have it, and what it means day to day.

## 1. Weight and space decide how many vans a delivery needs

**What happened.** About twelve years ago we over-specced the equipment for the space in one van and
tried to deliver to several London venues in a single overloaded run. It should have been two vans,
probably three.

**The rule.** For any delivery, keep track of the weight and space the job needs against the vehicle.
If the client adds more kit or more stops, tell them straight away if it's likely to change the
timings, the price, or both.

**Why.** Being knowledgeable and upfront about what a change will mean is always better than finding
out on the day.

## 2. A booking is secured by payment

**The rule.** Nearly every booking (99%) is secured with payment up front. A quote is not a booking.

**Warning signs to spot early:** constant late payment, or dragging heels over decisions. Sometimes
that's genuine; sometimes it means they're playing us against other companies for the cheapest price.
Either way, don't hold kit or vans on goodwill. Hold provisionally for the agreed time, then ask for
the deposit.

## 3. Explain the van insurance excess early and plainly

**What clients get wrong.** The excess is the most common sticking point every week: when it's due,
how it's paid, and when it comes back.

**The rule.** Explain it before they ask, in plain words:
- It's £1,000 + VAT (£1,200). Our insurers don't offer a reduction or waiver.
- It's due on or before the first day of the hire, by card, bank transfer, cash or PayPal.
- It usually comes back within ten days of the last hire day, unless we need to claim for
  under-fuelling, new damage or a third-party claim.

## 4. Clients don't know we supply both

**What clients get wrong.** People asking about a van often don't realise we supply backline, and
the other way round.

**The rule.** If a client mentions getting something we supply from somewhere else ("we're picking
our backline up from X"), offer to quote for it.

## For Claude

- When a reply touches delivery or collection and the client adds kit or stops, say it may affect
  the timings or price and that we'll confirm. Never say it will definitely fit.
- Never describe a quote as a booking. A booking is secured by the deposit (or full payment for a
  single day).
- When the excess comes up, or a van client seems unsure about money, explain it in the three points
  above, briefly.
- If the client mentions sourcing backline or a van elsewhere, offer in one line to quote for it.
